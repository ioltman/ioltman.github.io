How to read the geometry

The spectral curve and its logarithmic lift

The plots show several projections of one complex one-dimensional—hence real two-dimensional—surface. No single three-dimensional picture preserves all four real coordinates, so the views are linked.

1. The curve

For fixed \(E\) and \(\lambda\), the classical energy surface is

\[\Gamma_E=\left\{(z,p)\in(\mathbb C/2\pi\mathbb Z)^2:\ \cos p+\lambda\cos z=E\right\}.\]

Exponentiating the angular variables, \(X=e^{iz}\) and \(Y=e^{ip}\), gives the algebraic equation

\[Y+Y^{-1}+\lambda\bigl(X+X^{-1}\bigr)=2E.\]

Its compactification is generically an elliptic curve. The unrestricted logarithmic momentum is the infinite lift \(p\mapsto p+2\pi k\) above this compact quotient.

2. Two sheets and turning points

Over a regular point \(z\), choose the principal value

\[p_{\mathrm{pr}}(z)=\arccos\!\bigl(E-\lambda\cos z\bigr).\]

Every momentum above \(z\) is then

\[p(z)=\pm p_{\mathrm{pr}}(z)+2\pi k,\qquad k\in\mathbb Z.\]

The two algebraic sheets meet where \(\sin p=0\), equivalently where \(E-\lambda\cos z=\pm1\). Red points have \(\cos p=1\); blue points have \(\cos p=-1\).

3. Lifting a contour

Let \(\gamma:[0,1]\to\mathbb C/2\pi\mathbb Z\) be a contour in the \(z\)-plane. Once \(p(0)\) is chosen, analytic continuation determines a unique continuous lift \((\gamma(s),p(s))\in\Gamma_E\), provided the contour does not pass through a turning point.

Numerically, at consecutive samples \(z_j\), the tool chooses the candidate satisfying

\[p_j=\underset{\sigma=\pm1,\ k\in\mathbb Z}{\operatorname*{argmin}} \left|\sigma p_{\mathrm{pr}}(z_j)+2\pi k-p_{j-1}\right|.\]

Thus the plotted lift stays continuous. A blue/orange change only says that its description in the principal \(\arccos\) chart changed.

4. What a branch switch means

Near a simple turning point \(z_*\), the local coordinate has square-root form

\[p-p_*\sim c\sqrt{z-z_*}.\]

Going once around \(z_*\) reverses the square root. Consequently, a closed lifted contour has one of the two outcomes

\[p(1)=p(0)+2\pi k\qquad\text{or}\qquad p(1)=-p(0)+2\pi k.\]

The second relation is a genuine sheet exchange. Encircling one simple turning point produces it; encircling two generally returns to the original sheet.

5. The elliptic double cover

With \(t=\cos z\) and \(q=\sin z\sin p\), the same curve becomes

\[q^2=(1-t^2)\left(1-(E-\lambda t)^2\right).\]

It is a two-sheeted cover of the \(t\)-sphere branched over

\[-1,\quad 1,\quad \frac{E-1}{\lambda},\quad \frac{E+1}{\lambda}.\]

Four distinct branch points give genus one. At \(E=\pm1\pm\lambda\), two collide and one cycle pinches; these are precisely the four corners of \(\Sigma_\lambda\).

The two displayed copies are the \(q=\pm\sqrt{(1-t^2)(1-(E-\lambda t)^2)}\) sheets relative to the drawn cuts. They are not the same labels as \(\pm p_{\mathrm{pr}}\). A lifted path changes displayed copy exactly when its \(t\)-projection crosses a dotted cut.

6. Reading the linked windows

  • \(z\)-plane: dotted curves satisfy \(\operatorname{Im}(E-\lambda\cos z)=0\); orange subarcs have real momentum. Solid green arrows follow \((\operatorname{Im}p,\operatorname{Re}p)\), and dashed purple arrows follow \((-\operatorname{Im}p,\pi-\operatorname{Re}p)\). Along every flow line, momentum is analytically continued to the nearest lift \(\pm p_{\mathrm{pr}}+2\pi k\). The drawn contour is coloured by its current principal-sheet label.
  • \(p\)-plane and trace: these show the continuous lifted momentum. Purple dashed lines mark chart relabellings.
  • 3D + colour: space records \((\operatorname{Re}z,-\operatorname{Im}z,-\operatorname{Im}p)\), while colour records \(\operatorname{Re}p\bmod2\pi\).
  • Branched cover: the lifted contour moves between two copies of the \(t\)-plane when it crosses a cut.
  • Amoeba/coamoeba: these are the exact projections \((-\operatorname{Im}z,-\operatorname{Im}p)\) and \((\operatorname{Re}z,\operatorname{Re}p)\bmod2\pi\).
Differentiating the logarithmic actions produces the holomorphic elliptic differential \[\Omega=-\frac{dz}{\sin p}=\frac{dt}{q},\qquad \frac{\partial P}{\partial E}=\oint_A\Omega,\qquad \frac{\partial Z}{\partial E}=\oint_B\Omega.\] The two independent cycles of the elliptic curve are therefore the geometric source of the two actions \(P\) and \(Z\).
